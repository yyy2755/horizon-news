## [Radical Interpretation Meets Mechanistic Interpretability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26523) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A new paper proposes a framework that combines radical interpretation philosophy with mechanistic interpretability tools to systematically attribute beliefs, desires, and meanings to AI systems. This work addresses a critical gap in AI safety by providing a principled method for understanding whether AI systems are truthful or deceptive, which is essential for trusting deployed models. The framework emphasizes holism: beliefs, desires, and propositional structure must be jointly constrained, and piecemeal attribution can introduce distortions. It ties philosophical criteria to tests that current interpretability methods can perform.

rss · ArXiv ML (cs.AI) · Jun 26, 04:00

**Background**: Radical interpretation, introduced by Donald Davidson, is a philosophical approach to interpreting a speaker's language and mental states from scratch, without prior knowledge of their beliefs or meanings. Mechanistic interpretability aims to reverse-engineer neural networks by analyzing their internal computations, such as using sparse autoencoders to extract features. This paper bridges these two fields to create a rigorous foundation for attributing propositional attitudes to AI.

## [AutoCog: AI Automates Psychological Theory Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26448) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers introduced AutoCog, an autonomous AI system that uses LLM agents to propose, test, and refine psychological theories through closed-loop experimentation, recovering known decision-making strategies and discovering a novel theory of multi-cue decision-making. AutoCog addresses a fundamental bottleneck in cognitive science by automating theory generation, which has remained manual, potentially accelerating scientific discovery and enabling cumulative theory building. AutoCog uses LLM agents to advocate competing theories expressed as executable cognitive models, designs experiments that discriminate them, collects human behavioral data online, and synthesizes better successor theories. In human experiments, it produced theories outperforming established ones and generalized to held-out studies.

rss · ArXiv ML (cs.AI) · Jun 26, 04:00

**Background**: Cognitive science has automated data collection, modeling, and experiment design, but theory generation—the creative step of improving models—has remained manual. AutoCog closes this loop by integrating LLM agents that can generate, test, and refine theories autonomously, similar to how closed-loop experimentation is used in other sciences like chemistry.
