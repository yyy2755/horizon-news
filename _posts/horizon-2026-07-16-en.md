## [AI advice reduces willingness to say 'I don't know'](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13562) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A new study with five experiments (N=3,132) shows that access to AI advice, even when wrong, nearly eliminates people's willingness to say 'I don't know', leading to overconfidence and worse accuracy. This research reveals a critical cognitive side effect of AI assistants: they suppress metacognitive judgment, potentially harming decision-making in high-stakes domains like medicine or law. Participants answered more questions with AI but were correct about a third as often, while their confidence nearly doubled. Incentivizing accuracy reduced but did not eliminate the effect.

rss · ArXiv ML (cs.AI) · Jul 16, 04:00

**Background**: The study used difficult questions where AI advice was deliberately wrong to isolate the effect of AI presence from accuracy. 'I don't know' is a fundamental metacognitive judgment that signals uncertainty.

**Background**: Microsoft Comic Chat was a graphical IRC client first released with Internet Explorer 3.0 in 1996. IRC (Internet Relay Chat) is a text-based chat protocol that predates modern messaging apps.

The author notes that for compilers emitting machine code, memory-unsafe operations are a big part of the job, but community members like steveklabnik argue this is only true for specific features like hot patching.
