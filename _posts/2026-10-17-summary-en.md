## [Solar surpasses nuclear power globally for first time](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/29/solar-surpasses-nuclear-power-globally-for-first-time/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Global solar generation has surpassed nuclear power for the first time, with solar capacity growing 27% year-on-year to 2,383 GW while nuclear capacity rose only 1.5 GW to 399.5 GW. Wind is also nearing nuclear output, and from March 2025 through June 2026 either solar or wind individually generated more electricity than nuclear every month. This milestone signals a historic shift in the global power mix, as rapidly falling costs and record deployment of renewables outpace stagnant nuclear growth. It affects energy policy, grid planning, and climate strategy worldwide, highlighting the accelerating transition away from fossil fuels. Solar's lead is notable because nuclear has a much higher capacity factor, meaning it generates more electricity per unit of installed capacity; solar's rapid growth has overcome this advantage. The next major milestone, solar overtaking hydroelectric generation, is still several years away at current growth rates.

hackernews · xbmcuser · Oct 17, 14:01 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50131256)

**Background**: Capacity factor is the ratio of actual electricity generated over a period to the maximum possible output if a plant ran at full capacity continuously; nuclear plants typically have high capacity factors (often above 90%), while solar and wind have lower ones due to variability. Global solar and wind generation have been growing rapidly, with renewables recently overtaking coal in some periods, driven by cost declines and policy support.

By providing pre-purchase estimates, it could save users from costly mistakes and make local AI more accessible. The tool likely uses benchmark data to model performance, but community members note that real-world workloads with long prompts (60k-140k tokens) and cache-aware inference can see decode throughput drop to half or a third of benchmark numbers. Parameter tuning and inference engine choice (e.g., vLLM vs llama.cpp) also significantly affect actual speed.

Tools like Llmspeed aim to bridge this gap by providing estimates based on hardware specifications.

## [Claude Code Team Advocates Continuous AI Code Review Over Human Gate](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/16/code-review/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

In a blog post, Anthropic's Claude Code team members Addy Osmani, Morgan Westlee Lunt, and Qing Wang argue that the question 'did a human read the code?' is an imprecise proxy for assessing risk, and that review should instead run continuously with Claude reviewing most changes before a human looks at them. This signals a paradigm shift in engineering workflows: instead of treating code review as a final human gate, AI-assisted continuous review catches routine mistakes early and frees human attention for higher-level design and risk questions, potentially reshaping how teams staff and structure review processes. The team describes review now running 'like spellcheck while you write,' with most changes already reviewed and fixed by Claude at least once before a human is asked to look; the approach targets 'what could go wrong with this change, and which check—automated or human—is the right one to catch it?' rather than a binary human-read proxy.

rss · Simon Willison · Oct 16, 22:06

**Background**: Claude Code is Anthropic's agentic coding tool that can read a codebase, edit files, and run commands across terminal, IDE, and web surfaces. Traditionally, code review is a pull-request gate where a colleague must approve changes before merge. AI code review tools such as Greptile, Qodo, and Codacy already scan pull requests for bugs and vulnerabilities, but the Claude Code team's argument extends this to continuous, in-flow review rather than a final checkpoint.

## [Bleached corals recover within days when touching healthy clones](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03266-x) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A new laboratory study reported in Nature shows that bleached coral fragments can regain their symbiotic algae and color within days when placed in direct contact with a healthy coral neighbor. The healthy coral forms a tissue bridge and transfers algal symbionts and nutrients to the bleached fragment, with three days of contact being the minimum for recovery to begin and five days sufficient for full recovery. Coral reefs have declined by roughly 50% over the past 50 years, largely due to warming-driven bleaching, so a mechanism for rapid recovery could inform reef restoration strategies. However, the effect appears limited to genetically identical clones, which constrains its practical use in the wild. The experiments found that at least three days of contact were needed for recovery to begin, while five days ensured full recovery; the rescue works through tissue fusion between clonal fragments rather than between genetically distinct corals. This means the finding may be most useful for clonal corals that reproduce by fragmentation and form clusters of genetically identical individuals.

hackernews · alkyon · Oct 16, 23:38 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50126253)

**Background**: Coral bleaching occurs when heat stress causes corals to expel their symbiotic algae (Symbiodiniaceae), which provide them with nutrients and color; bleached corals can look lifeless but the animal inside may still be alive. Chimerism, the fusion of genetically distinct or identical coral tissues, has been studied as a strategy that may improve resilience in clonal invertebrates. This study demonstrates that healthy corals can actively transfer symbionts to bleached neighbors through direct tissue contact.

## [Substack post makes chicken breast transparent using Dorito dust](https://chillphysicsenjoyer.substack.com/p/making-chicken-transparent-using) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A Substack post demonstrates how to make raw chicken breast transparent by marinating it in a solution containing Dorito dust, likely leveraging the food dye tartrazine (Yellow 5). The experiment was inspired by recent scientific studies showing that the same dye can temporarily make mouse skin transparent. This playful experiment brings cutting-edge tissue-clearing research into the home kitchen, making food science accessible and sparking curiosity about the optical properties of everyday ingredients. It also highlights the potential of common food dyes in biomedical imaging and could inspire further citizen-science explorations. The transparency effect is likely due to the dye's ability to match the refractive index of tissue components, reducing light scattering. The author notes the difficulty of slicing raw chicken thinly; commenters suggest partially freezing the meat or using a sous vide bath for easier handling.

hackernews · crescit_eundo · Oct 17, 13:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50131213)

**Background**: In 2024, researchers discovered that applying a solution of tartrazine (Yellow 5), a common food dye found in Doritos and other snacks, can temporarily make mouse skin transparent by reducing light scattering. This effect, known as tissue clearing, works because the dye matches the refractive index of lipids and proteins, allowing light to pass through. The technique has potential applications in medical imaging and research, and the Substack post extends this concept to a kitchen experiment with chicken breast.
